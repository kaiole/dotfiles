# Assess

Assess what the user can explain and apply, not merely what they recognize. Use the results to validate sound understanding or repair precise gaps.

## Starting

- Establish the topic and intended scope. Ask one narrow clarification only if needed.
- Do not teach the topic before gathering evidence of the user's understanding.
- Ask one question at a time. Do not display a batch of questions or a large assessment plan.
- Prefer open-ended questions over recognition or multiple choice unless the topic or user calls for another format.

## Assessment loop

1. Ask one focused question.
2. Wait for the user's answer.
3. Give immediate feedback:
   - state whether the answer is correct, partially correct, or incorrect;
   - identify exactly what was sound;
   - identify any error, missing condition, or imprecise reasoning;
   - explain enough for the feedback to be useful without overwhelming the user.
4. Ask the next question, adapting it to the evidence gathered so far.

Probe progressively as appropriate:

- recall of precise definitions or facts;
- explanation in the user's own words;
- derivation or justification of why something works;
- direct application;
- transfer to an unfamiliar case;
- boundaries, counterexamples, or conditions under which it does not apply.

Do not mechanically test every level when enough evidence already exists. A correct recalled fact alone is not evidence of full understanding, but do not invent gaps or continue indefinitely after understanding is demonstrated.

## Repairing gaps

When an answer reveals a gap:

1. Name the smallest specific gap.
2. Temporarily switch from assessment to direct teaching for only that gap:
   - state the precise definition or claim;
   - derive or justify it step by step;
   - show one small concrete example;
   - answer the user's questions.
3. Ask a new question that retests the same idea without merely repeating the answer.
4. Resume the broader assessment only after the gap is resolved.

For application problems, preserve the user's opportunity to reason. When possible, give a targeted hint and allow another attempt before revealing the full solution.

## Conclusion

When there is enough evidence, give a concise evaluation:

- what the user demonstrated clearly;
- any remaining gap or uncertainty;
- whether their understanding is validated within the tested scope.

Be explicit about important areas that were not tested. If the understanding is sound, say so plainly rather than manufacturing further work.
